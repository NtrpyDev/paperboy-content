Key idea

Two Lipschitz functions on a Hilbert space always share a smooth point, but three need not.

The question

Functions with corners still have smooth spots.

A Lipschitz function can bend sharply, yet in ordinary finite-dimensional space it has a derivative almost everywhere. In a Hilbert space with infinitely many directions, Lindenstrauss, Preiss, and Tišer asked a longstanding question: must several such functions share a point where all are Fréchet differentiable.

What they did

A direct construction, with elementary tools.

The proof builds the three functions explicitly. It relies on elementary calculus and finite-dimensional linear algebra.

What they found

The answer is no, and three is the edge.

On every infinite-dimensional real Hilbert space, three real-valued Lipschitz functions can have no common point of Fréchet differentiability. Every Lipschitz map into the plane has such a point, so the target dimension three is optimal.
